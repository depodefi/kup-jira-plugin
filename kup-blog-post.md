# Afraid of 50% KUP? You Shouldn't Be — Here's How to Give Engineers a Raise That Costs You Nothing

If your company employs software engineers, R&D staff, or other creative professionals in Poland, you've probably heard three letters whispered with a mix of excitement and dread: **KUP**.

The excitement is justified. The dread isn't — at least not once you understand what's actually going on. This post is for the CFOs, People leaders, and engineering managers at international and Silicon Valley-style companies who keep hearing that "50% KUP" is some kind of tax minefield, and who'd rather understand it than avoid it.

The short version: 50% KUP is one of the few tax incentives in Poland built specifically to reward creative work. Used correctly, it puts meaningfully more money in your employees' pockets **at no extra cost to you**. The only real risk comes from running it sloppily — and that risk is entirely manageable with the right process and the right tooling.

## What 50% KUP actually is

Poland's tax code lets creative employees deduct a much higher cost of earning income than a standard employment contract allows. Under a normal contract, the deductible cost is a flat, almost symbolic amount — currently just **250 PLN per month**. For work that qualifies as creative (think software, R&D, design, technical authorship), the law permits **50% tax-deductible costs** on the portion of salary tied to that creative output.

The lawmaker's intent was straightforward: support creative and innovative work — explicitly including IT and R&D — by letting these employees keep more of what they earn.

The result is one of those rare win-win mechanics. For the **same total cost to the company**, a qualifying employee can take home a significantly higher net salary. We're talking about an average increase of around **15%**, and up to roughly **38,000 PLN in additional net pay per year** at the upper end.

Read that again, because it's the part that gets lost in the anxiety: you are not paying more. The employee simply keeps more of the salary you were already going to pay. In a competitive hiring market, that's one of the most cost-efficient retention and recruiting levers available to you.

## So why the fear?

Because Polish tax authorities have grown noticeably more interested in these pay models in recent years, and that scrutiny shows no sign of easing. Inspectors increasingly want to see whether a company has set the model up correctly and is running it the way the rules require.

When a model is set up carelessly, an inspection can be painful. Worst-case consequences include having to correct payrolls and pay back taxes going several years into the past, and in extreme cases personal exposure for the people responsible for payroll, finance, and tax matters inside the company.

That sounds scary in the abstract. But notice what's actually being penalized: not *using* 50% KUP, but using it **without proper documentation, verification, and records**. The benefit is legitimate and intended. The audits aren't hunting for companies that offer it — they're catching companies that can't prove they ran it properly.

That's a process problem. And process problems have process solutions.

## Where things actually go wrong (and how to stay clean)

The mistakes that get companies into trouble tend to cluster into a handful of predictable areas. None of them are mysterious, and each has a well-established best practice.

**Implementation errors.** Applying 50% KUP to roles or work that don't meet the creative-work criteria, treating it as automatic for an entire job rather than for genuine creative output, or skipping documentation when roles change. *The fix:* clear policies, role-by-role eligibility rules, proper contracts, and onboarding/training so everyone understands what qualifies.

**Calculation errors.** Paying the creative-work portion during periods when no creative work was actually delivered (vacation, sick leave), or basing the split on guesswork instead of evidence. *The fix:* tie the qualifying amount to documented creative output, and verify the calculation each period rather than carrying numbers forward blindly.

**Employee reporting errors.** Employees submitting work that isn't genuinely creative, or re-submitting the same work twice. *The fix:* a clear submission step plus verification by someone qualified to judge whether the work actually qualifies.

**Archiving errors.** This is the big one. Authorities increasingly ask companies to produce evidence of the specific works created in specific months — sometimes years later. If you can't retrieve it, you can't defend it. Improper or missing archiving makes that impossible. *The fix:* a reliable, per-month record of created works, with verified entries that can't quietly disappear, so any given month can be reconstructed on demand.

**Acceptance errors.** Approving creative work without any real review, or having approvers who don't understand the rules. *The fix:* a defined acceptance process and trained reviewers, so approval means something.

Across every one of these, the same theme repeats: **you need a defensible, time-stamped record of who created what, when, and that it was reviewed.** Get that right and the audit becomes a formality. Get it wrong and even a legitimate program becomes a liability.

## Where the plugin comes in

Here's the practical problem: the creative work that qualifies for 50% KUP is overwhelmingly done in Jira. Engineers live there. But the *evidence* of that work — the hours, the monthly attribution, the approvals, the audit trail — usually lives somewhere else entirely: spreadsheets, email threads, a shared drive nobody maintains. That gap between where work happens and where it's documented is exactly where archiving and verification errors are born.

Our Jira plugin closes that gap by keeping the compliance record right next to the work itself.

It lets administrators define which projects, issue types, and months are in scope, so eligibility rules are enforced rather than assumed. On each issue, a compliance panel records the qualifying work and writes it to a per-issue audit trail — every entry time-stamped and attributable. A reporting page aggregates each person's compliance hours by month, turning "can you prove what was created in March?" from a frantic search into a single query. Because the data is stored as indexed properties on the issues themselves, the monthly record is structured, queryable, and lives where your engineers already are — no parallel paperwork, no end-of-quarter reconstruction.

In other words, it operationalizes exactly the best practices that keep companies out of trouble: documented creative output, per-month archiving, verifiable records, and a defensible audit trail — built into the tool your team already uses every day.

## The takeaway

50% KUP is not a threat to be avoided. It's a genuine, government-intended benefit that lets you reward your most valuable creative talent without increasing your payroll cost. The companies that get burned aren't the ones who offer it — they're the ones who run it without records to back it up.

You don't have to choose between the upside and the safety. Offer the benefit, run it properly, and keep the evidence where the work happens. That's the whole game — and it's a very winnable one.
